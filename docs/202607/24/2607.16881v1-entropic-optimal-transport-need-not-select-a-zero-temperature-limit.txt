# Entropic optimal transport need not select a zero-temperature limit 

## Maja Gwóźdź ETH Zürich 

## mgwozdz@ethz.ch 

Abstract 

We construct a compact metric space with an atomless probability measure and a bounded Lipschitz cost for which the entropic optimal-transport minimisers have no zero-temperature weak limit. More precisely, Pε does not converge as ε ↓ 0. In the example, every unregularised minimiser is singular with respect to μ ⊗ μ, so that the entropy on the optimal face is identically 

+∞. We describe the cluster set by 

Clust( Pε) = {Pw : w ∈ W} ,

where Pw is the mixture of the two zero-cost graph couplings with weight w, and where W ⊂ [0 , 1] 

is a non-degenerate compact interval. We then compute two explicit points w− < w + in this interval. This shows that compactness, atomlessness, and Lipschitz regularity of the cost do not imply zero-temperature convergence. We also present a compactness theorem for the general problem. If C ∈ L1(μ ⊗ ν) is continuous and bounded from below on Polish spaces, then the zero-temperature cluster set is a nonempty weakly compact connected subset of the optimal face. In the proof, we apply the cluster-point theorem of Bernton, Ghosal, and Nutz and the continuity of ε 7 → πε. Finally, we give local and exterior first-order criteria for full convergence and cluster membership. We show that nonconvergence is possible, but only through a connected continuum of optimal plans.

Keywords. Entropic optimal transport, Schrödinger bridge, zero-temperature limit, nonconvergence, selection principle, cluster set.

1arXiv:2607.16881v1 [math.OC] 18 Jul 2026 For the related background, consult [ 15 , 11 ]. The limit ε ↓ 0 is a selection problem on the optimal face of the unregularised transport problem. The first natural question is whether πε converges. If it does, one may ask which optimal plan is selected. In this work, we prove that convergence can fail even in a compact atomless bounded-Lipschitz setting. We first establish the general topological restriction: the zero-temperature cluster set is always a nonempty compact connected subset of the optimal face. The optimality of cluster points follows from [ 2, Proposition 3.2], while connectedness follows from weak compactness and continuity of the entropic curve. We then construct a dyadic Cantor-group model in which this connected set is nontrivial. The mechanism is an alternation of scales, analogous to chaotic temperature dependence for zero-temperature Gibbs states [ 21 , 7]. Here, the oscillating object is the Schrödinger projection under fixed marginal constraints.

## 1.2 Related work 

The entropy-to-transport limit and the connection with the Schrödinger problem are studied in Léonard [ 14 , 15 ]. Nutz and Wiesel [ 18 ] prove compactness and convergence results for Schrödinger potentials, and in one dimension with distance cost, Di Marino and Louet [ 12 ] determine the selected plan under additional assumptions on the marginals. See also Cominetti and San Martín [ 9] for the finite-dimensional analogue and selecting a point of the optimal face. In linear programming, Weed [ 22 ] established direct asymptotic estimates for the entropic penalty in linear programming. Other relevant works study convergence of entropic schemes [ 5], convergence rates [ 6, 20 ], stability [ 13 ], regularity of the Schrödinger cost [ 8 ], displacement smoothness [ 4], and entropic approximation of ∞-optimal transport problems [ 3]. Certain recent selection results impose additional geometry. For instance, Ley [ 16 ]analyses the line with distance cost, while Nutz and Zhong [ 19 ] study c(x, y ) = ∥x − y∥ in dimensions d > 1.Aryan and Ghosal [ 1] prove related ray-wise entropy limits for the Euclidean distance cost, and Yun [ 23 ]obtains the Gaussian selection result. On compact metric spaces with Lipschitz costs, Mengue [ 17 ] developed a zero-temperature pressure formulation. This result recovers the Monge–Kantorovich problem and its duality in the limit, but it does not constitute a selection theorem for the entropic minimisers in (1.1). 

## 1.3 Idea of the proof 

We first prove the compactness theorem. In the proof, we use weak compactness of the coupling set, continuity of ε 7 → πε, and optimality of zero-temperature cluster points. More precisely, it represents Clust (πε) as a nested intersection of compact connected sets. We then reduce the counterexample to a scalar oscillation. We set H := {s ∈ G : s1 = 0 }, and prove that the diagonally H-invariant part of the zero-cost face is exactly 

{ wP 0 + (1 − w)Pt0 : 0 ≤ w ≤ 1 }

and has singular zero-cost couplings. At positive temperature, the Schrödinger system then reduces to a 2 × 2

Sinkhorn scaling problem. The two different subsequential limits in the branch weight follow from the scales 

αn = e−2n

and the alternating factors Ln. Once we have identified all weak cluster points with the points on the segment, we apply the connectedness theorem to turn those two limits into a non-degenerate interval. The paper is organised as follows. In Section 2, we recall the compactness and lower-semicontinuity facts for entropic minimisers. In Section 3, we present the connectedness theorem, and in Sections 4–6, we analyse the dyadic Cantor-group construction. In Appendix A, we prove the local and exterior variational criteria. Finally, in Appendix B, we study the scaling algebra, and in Appendix C, we classify the diagonally 

H-invariant zero-cost couplings.

We apply two results of Bernton–Ghosal–Nutz [ 2, Propositions 2.2 and 3.2]. In their notation, for a nonnegative continuous cost c, the first result states that, under the finite-value condition

# A Variational criteria for cluster membership and full convergence
