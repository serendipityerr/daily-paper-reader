# Optimal Covariance Estimates for Schr¨ odinger Semigroups with White Noise in d “ 1, 2

Youssef Djellouli ∗ Pierre Yves Gaudreau Lamarre †

Abstract 

For d P t 1, 2u, let H “ ´ 12 ∆ ` V ` ξ be the random Schr¨ odinger operator on 

L2pRdq where ξ is a standard Gaussian white noise and V is a deterministic potential with power-law growth at infinity. Using a Feynman-Kac formula for the trace of the Schr¨ odinger semigroup, we give optimal asymptotic upper and lower bounds on the covariance of Tr re´sH s and Tr re´tH s as s, t Ñ 0 through estimates on Brownian bridge local times. These estimates are a significant improvement on previous bounds in the case d “ 1 and are the first of their kind for d “ 2. As an application of these new estimates, we prove a quantitative hyperuniformity-type property and decorrelation rate for the trace as s, t Ñ 0.

In physical terms, this consists of studying the energy levels of an isolated quantum particle that is subjected to the confining potential V and impurities/disorder modelled by ξ.The motivation for using a white noise perturbation in (1.1) can in part be explained by the fact that ξ is the universal scaling limit of i.i.d. lattice noises with finite variance (as the spacing between lattice points vanishes). Thus, ξ is arguably one of the most natural models of unstructured noise on Rd, and can be viewed as a continuum analogue of Anderson’s celebrated tight-binding model [And58]. However, despite this natural interest, most rigorous results concerning these models are quite recent, especially for d ě 2. Much of the reason for this has to do with the fact that Gaussian white noise is highly singular (in fact, a Schwartz distribution), which makes the rigorous definition and study of operators of the form (1.1) technically challenging. More specifically, for d “ 1, the rigorous theory of Schr¨ odinger operators with white noise was pioneered by Fukushima and Nakao [FN77]. Therein, they showed how the operator 

´12 ∆ ` ξ on the bounded interval r0, L s (with Dirichlet boundary conditions) can be con-structed through its quadratic form, wherein integrals against ξ are interpreted as stochastic integrals. They then used their construction to study the integrated density of states of the operator on large intervals, thus confirming a physics prediction due to Frisch, Halperin, and Lloyd [FL60, Hal65]. Over the past 30 years, the spectral properties of that same operator (such as eigenvalue tails and localization-to-delocalization transition) were studied in great detail in a series of works by various authors [CM99, CRR06, McK94, DL20, DL23a, DL23b, DL24]. Finally, more general one-dimensional Schr¨ odinger operators (e.g., with nonzero 

V ) with white noise were considered by various authors, motivated in part by applications in multivariate statistics and the study of simple interacting particle systems (namely, the 

β-ensembles); e.g., [BV13, BV16, DL22, GL21, LGL20, GLGLL23, Min15, Min24, RRV11]. For d ě 2, the rigorous construction of Schr¨ odinger operators with white noise is sub-stantially more difficult, and typically requires sophisticated renormalization theories, such as Martin Hairer’s regularity structures [Hai14] or the paracontrolled calculus of Gubinelli, Imkeller, and Perkowski [GIP15]. Using this technology, Allez and Chouk constructed the 3operator ´12 ∆ ` ξ on the two-dimensional torus [AC15], and studied the tails of its eigen-values. Then, Labb´ e constructed the same operator for d ď 3 on the torus or a box with Dirichlet boundary conditions [Lab19], and also studied the eigenvalue tails. Since then, other properties of ´12 ∆ ` ξ’s spectrum on various spaces and manifolds of dimension d ď 3were investigated, such as exact eigenvalue asymptotics, density of states, and spectral ge-ometry; e.g., [BDM25, CvZ21, GLP26, HL23, Mat22, MvZ25, Mou22]. 

Remark 1.1. To our knowledge, no Schr¨ odinger operators have been constructed with white noise in d ě 4. As explained in several papers (e.g. [Hai14, (PAM) and surrounding paragraph] or [CGP17, Paragraph between (2) and (3)]), this has to do with the balance between the scaling/regularity properties of the Laplacian and the white noise, whereby the noise “overpowers” the Laplacian when d ě 4 even if an additive renormalization is applied. This is consistent with two recent works concerning the d “ 4 case [DS26, GR26], which show that a logarithmic attenuation of the noise leads to nontrivial fluctuations in ´12 ∆ ` ξ’s Green function on a torus.

Remark 1.13. This equality is literal when d “ 1 since H is defined through its quadratic form. However, when d “ 2 the white noise requires a renormalization. In that case, the equality should be viewed as an informal heuristic. In order to minimize the first integral, the eigenfunctions are incentivized to spread out. On the other hand, in order to minimize the second integral, the eigenfunctions want to concentrate on the points where the potential and the noise are the smallest. In particular, eigenfunctions that concentrate in the same areas pick up the same noise from ξ and so this 10 competition between the two terms is what drives the eigenvalue correlations or lack thereof. Consequently, we expect a larger dimension to give the eigenfunctions ϕk more space to separate and hence decorrelate faster, whereas a stronger confining potential forces them to remain in a smaller area and “reuse” the same noise ξpxq, increasing their correlation. Corollary 1.12 demonstrates the precise quantitative effects of these competing phenomena when measuring eigenvalue correlations through the semigroup’s trace. 

Remark 1.14. In [BFK16] and [DJY26], a more literal manifestation of this mechanism was observed for lattice Hamiltonians with i.i.d. noise and ´12 ∆ ` ξ on a one-dimensional interval, respectively. More specifically, in [BFK16, (1.11)], we see that the above heuristic of correlations coming from “reusing” the same noise manifests quite literally as an eigenfunc-tion inner product; in [DJY26, Theorem 1.1], the random fluctuations of high eigenvalues manifest as a stochastic Rayleigh-Schr¨ odinger-type correction. Thus, Corollary 1.12 can be viewed as a first step toward exploring this mechanism in general Anderson models with nontrivial noise and coercive potential in the continuum. 

Remark 1.15. The phenomena observed in Corollary 1.12 and [BFK16, DJY26] are in sharp contrast with what is typically observed in full-space Anderson models with no deterministic potential (i.e. V “ 0), i.e., a complete decorrelation effect; see, e.g., [Klo11, GK14].
