# A Dacorogna-Moser construction of transport maps on Rd with application to geodesics on the space of couplings 

# Louis-Pierre Chaintron , Matteo Picco 

École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL), Institute of Mathematics, 1015 Lausanne, Switzerland. 

Abstract 

A seminal work by Dacorogna and Moser introduced a way of constructing reg-ular transport maps from a probability distribution on a bounded domain to an-other one. In this work, we extend this construction to the whole Rd for strictly asymptotically log-concave measures, a wide class of distributions that encompasses Lipschitz-perturbations of log-concave measures. We then leverage this construction to study geodesics in the space of probability measures on a product set with imposed marginal laws (couplings), for which we derive optimality conditions, answering an open question in a recent work by Conforti, Lacker and Pal. Taking inspiration from Brenier’s variational model for incompressible fluids and its regularization, we further introduce an entropic regularization of the geodesic problem, which can be seen as the Schrödinger bridge problem on the space of couplings, for which we also derive optimality conditions. We eventually study convergence of minimizers as the regularization vanishes, and we prove convergence of the related Lagrange multipli-ers. Our approach involves proving uniform-in-time global regularity estimates on elliptic and parabolic equations on Rd, by exploiting the structure of asymptotically log-concave measures using the probabilistic notion of reflection coupling. 

# 1. Introduction 

The construction of regular transport maps has attracted significant attention in recent years, motivated by both theoretical and applied considerations. On the theoretical side, such maps play a central role in extending functional inequalities, including the Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities, to broader classes of probability measures [KM12, MS23, FMS24]. From a compu-tational perspective, regular transport maps provide an effective framework for sampling from complex probability distributions [MMPS16, Che23]. Indeed, a Lipschitz transport map from a simple reference measure – such as a Gaussian – to a target measure enables sampling from the latter while providing explicit error estimates in terms of the Lipschitz constant of the map. This principle is fundamental in modern generative machine learning, where the objective is to efficiently generate samples from prescribed probability distributions. Many approaches have been developed to enlarge the class of target measures that can be handled and to improve the efficiency of sampling, including diffusion models [HJA20, SSDK +21], flow matching [LCBH +23] and stochastic interpolants [AVE23]. 1.2. Literature review 

Hereafter, we review several contributions related to problems discussed in this article. 

Transport maps on bounded domains. As previously mentioned, [DM90] showed a way to construct regular transport maps between two probability distributions that are supported on bounded sets. More precisely, given a bounded set Ω ⊂ Rd with a Ck+3 ,α boundary ∂Ω, for some integer k ≥ 0, α ∈ (0 , 1) , and functions f, g ∈ Ck,α (Ω) such that f, g > 0 on Ω and ´ f dx = ´ gdx, [DM90, Theorem 1] constructs a transport map T from f dx to gdx that fixes the boundary ∂Ω, and such that T, T −1 ∈ Ck+1 ,α (Ω , Rd). This result was later improved by relaxing the Hölder-continuity of densities in [RY96], allowing for weaker conditions such as bounded or BMO densities. In both these works, the dependence of the Hölder norms on the measure of the bounded set Ω is explicit, preventing any direct extension of the construction to the whole Rd. To our knowledge, Theorem 1.2 is the first such extension. Recalling Remark 1.3, we emphasize that the map T in Theorem 1.2 has one less degree of regularity than in the 5aforementioned works; however, our regularity estimates are global in space contrary to these works. In the recent literature, several strategies have been proposed to build transport maps on the whole Rd, one popular method relying on the time-reversal of diffusion processes.

The Langevin diffusion (10) has been further used in [FMS24, Theorem 1] to show existence for a bi-Lipschitz transport map from an α-log concave measure μ = e−U , α > 0, to any log-Lipschitz perturbation ν = e−U +V , i.e. V is Lipschitz-continuous, under an additional boundedness assumption on ∇3U . Regard-ing assumptions, we relax the strong log-concavity assumption on μ into the less restrictive requirement that μ is strictly asymptotically log-concave. However, our regularity assumptions are slightly stronger, as we require both ∇U and ∇2U to be Lipschitz – see Assumption (A.1) below. We also need to assume that the log-perturbation V is bounded and Lipschitz-continuous (φ = eV −1 in our notation), whereas [FMS24, Theorem 1] only requires V being Lipschitz. This is related to the fact that we consider target measures of the shape (1 + φ) · μ, and we control the Lipschitz constant directly in terms of φ, as it is more suitable for the geodesic problem studied in Section 2.2. We furthermore quantify the higher-order regularity of the transport map in terms of the one of φ.

Gaussian setting and Ornstein-Uhlenbeck diffusion. Various improvements have been pro-posed for the case where μ is the standard Gaussian, i.e. U (x) = 12 |x|2. [MS23, Theorem 1, Theorem 2] proves the Lipschitz-continuity of the heat flow map when the target measure ν is strongly log-concave, or when ν is the convolution of the standard Gaussian with a compactly supported measure. To do so, [MS23, Lemma 3] establishes an explicit bound on the Lipschitz constant, which depends on the derivative of the velocity field in (11) – also called the score function – thus reducing the problem to bounding −∇ 2 log( μt/μ ). This estimate is exploited 6in [CCE25, Theorem 1.4] to prove existence for a Lipschitz map from the standard Gaussian to any strictly asymptotically log-concave measure ν such that ´ 10 rκ −− log ν (r)d r < +∞, recalling the definition (4) of the weak semiconvexity profile. More generally, proving Lipschitz bounds on the score function for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process has become a common strategy, which is used in [ICB26, Theorem 1] to prove existence of bi-Lipschitz transport maps from the standard Gaussian to a general class of measures, which includes Gaussian mixtures and convolutions of Gaussians with compactly supported measures. Similarly, by studying the regularity of the score function, [Sté25, Theorem 2] proves that the heat flow map belongs to C⌊β⌋+1 when the target measure ν is a bounded β-Hölder perturbation of the standard Gaussian, allowing for quantifying the regularity of the map depending on the one of the perturbation. Our results in Theorem 1.2 are less precise than the aforementioned ones on Hölder-perturbations of Gaussians, but our setting is more general as it allows for any initial distribution μ that is strictly asymptotically log-concave – not necessarily a Gaussian. For the sake of completeness, we finally mention the works [CPZ25, CEP26], which prove quantitative stability for the heat-flow map with respect to the target distribution ν, in the Gaussian case.

Geodesics in the space of couplings. The basic references [CLP25, CL25] on the geodesic problem in Π( μ, ν ) have already been discussed in Section 1.1. We therefore focus here on related variational problems that motivate our analysis. Our study of Lagrange multipliers is inspired by Brenier’s multiphase formulation of incom-pressible Euler equations [Bre99]. In that setting, the pressure arises as the Lagrange multiplier associated with the incompressibility constraint and is shown to be unique as a distribution, with ∇p ∈ M ((0 , 1) × Td). The regularity theory was subsequently refined in [FA07], which proves that ∇p ∈ L2((0 , 1) , M(Td)) and p ∈ L2((0 , 1) , L d/ (d−1) (Td)) . Determining the optimal regularity of the pressure remains a challenging open question, see for instance the semiconcavity conjecture in [Bre13]. In contrast, our work establishes existence, uniqueness, and rigorous opti-mality conditions for the Lagrange multipliers associated with the marginal constraints, but does not address their optimal regularity. Whether or not the techniques of [FA07] can be adapted to our setting is an interesting open question. The regularized problem considered in this article is similarly motivated by the entropic reg-ularization of Brenier’s formulation. This latter regularization was introduced in [MACJC17] as the Brödinger problem ; it amounts to adding a Fisher-information term to the kinetic en-ergy – see Appendix A. In particular, this regularization restores uniqueness of the minimizer, which does not hold in general for the unregularized Brenier problem. Building on this formula-tion, [Bar20] establishes existence, uniqueness, and optimality conditions for the pressure field, while [BM20] proves Γ-convergence towards Brenier’s multiphase problem together with weak-⋆

convergence of the associated multipliers. Our approach follows the same general strategy, but substantial modifications are required. In particular, the heat-flow regularization used in [BM20] does not preserve the constraint set 

Π( μ, ν ) and therefore cannot be used to construct recovery sequences. Instead, assuming that 

∇2 log μ and ∇2 log ν are bounded, we regularize by the gradient flow of the relative entropy 

H(· | μ ⊗ ν) in (P2(Rd × Rd), W2), which leaves Π( μ, ν ) invariant. The required regularization estimates are obtained using the theory of the Schrödinger bridge problem on general metric spaces developed in [MTV23].

This statement can be made rigorous using the (probabilistic) Girsanov theory under finite entropy condition [Léo12] – although this goes beyond the scope of the present work. We refer to [Léo13] for a rigorous discussion of such pathwise formulations in the context of the Schrödinger bridge problem, and to [MACJC17] and [Bar20, Section 3] in the context of the Brödinger problem. We finally emphasize the convenience of the entropic formulation (52), which directly benefits from the nice properties of the relative entropy – such as lower semicontinuity, strict convexity and compactness of level sets –, although (15) was more adequate to derive a complete PDE description of optimality conditions.
