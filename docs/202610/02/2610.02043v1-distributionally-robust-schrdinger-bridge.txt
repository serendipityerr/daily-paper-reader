## ABSTRACT 

Schrödinger bridge (SB) learns stochastic transport between prescribed initial and target distributions. When the initial distribution shifts at test time, the learned dy-namics can fail to recover the target distribution. We introduce the Distributionally Robust Schrödinger Bridge (DRSB), which learns a single controller that accounts for uncertainty in the initial distribution. The DRSB objective consists of control energy and a KL penalty between the resulting terminal distribution and the target distribution. DRSB seeks a single controller that minimizes the worst-case value of this objective as the initial distribution varies within an ambiguity set around the nominal distribution. We derive an exact variational formulation of this objective and connect its fixed-terminal-cost subproblem to stochastic optimal control and distributionally robust optimization. This formulation motivates an alternating algorithm that updates the adversarial initial distribution, estimates the terminal log-density ratio, and trains the controller. We develop Wasserstein and Sinkhorn variants using stochastic control optimality conditions to approximate the gradients required for adversarial updates. Experiments on two-dimensional transport tasks and image-to-image translation show improved robustness to input perturbations relative to standard SB, with a tradeoff in nominal performance. On Gaussian mixture transport, Sinkhorn DRSB also achieves lower mean sliced Wasserstein distance than fixed-level noise augmentation at both tested unseen noise levels. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Generative modeling is fundamentally a task of learning a transport map from a source distribution to a target distribution. While early approaches, such as GANs (Goodfellow et al., 2014), focused on learning static maps, recent approaches such as diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021) learn dynamic transport maps via stochastic differential equations (SDEs). The Schrödinger Bridge (SB) problem generalizes standard diffusion models and likewise seeks an optimal transport mapping described by an SDE. By definition, SB seeks a stochastic process that connects two arbitrary distributions and minimizes the KL divergence with respect to a reference stochastic process (Schrödinger, 1931; Léonard, 2014). SB has been used in applications such as sampling (Liu et al., 2025), image-to-image translation (Theodoropoulos et al., 2024), and image restoration (Liu et al., 2023). However, the SB dynamics are trained for a prescribed initial distribution. When this distribution shifts at test time, the same dynamics produce a different terminal distribution, violating the original target marginal constraint. This motivates a distributionally robust SB formulation that accounts for uncertainty in the initial distribution. In this paper, we formulate the Distributionally Robust Schrödinger Bridge (DRSB) problem to achieve robust generation under initial distribution shifts. The goal of DRSB is to find a single controller that transports every initial distribution in a prescribed set close to the target distribution. This set, called the ambiguity set, consists of distributions within a prescribed distance of the nominal initial distribution. We connect DRSB to stochastic optimal control through a terminal cost that penalizes mismatch between the induced terminal distribution and the target distribution. This cost takes the form of the log-density ratio between these distributions, which we approximate with a 1arXiv:2610.02043v1 [cs.LG] 1 Oct 2026 neural network to provide a training signal for the controller. Since the induced terminal distribution depends on both the controller and the initial distribution, the terminal cost must be re-estimated as these change. For a fixed terminal cost, the resulting subproblem has the standard distributionally robust optimization (DRO) form (Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn, 2018; Gao & Kleywegt, 2023), where the control variable serves as the DRO decision variable. Strong duality results from the DRO literature provide a dual formulation of the inner worst-case maximization over the ambiguity set. We update the adversarial initial distribution using this dual formulation and train the controller via adjoint matching (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2024) to minimize the expected transport cost under the current adversarial distribution. We alternate these updates with re-estimation of the terminal cost, training its network as a binary classifier that distinguishes the resulting terminal samples from target samples. We develop Wasserstein and Sinkhorn variants of DRSB, with adversarial input updates derived from their DRO dual formulations. Using an optimality condition from stochastic optimal control theory (Kappen, 2005), we approximate cost gradients with controller evaluations. The Wasserstein variant iteratively updates adversarial inputs. For the Sinkhorn variant, a first-order approximation of the cost yields a Gaussian sampling rule for each nominal input and a closed-form update of the dual variable. This approximation allows adversarial inputs to be sampled without iterative inner optimization. We evaluate DRSB on 2D Gaussian-to-Gaussian and GMM-to-GMM transport tasks and on image-to-image translation. The results show that DRSB improves robustness to noisy and shifted initial distributions compared with standard SB. On the 2D transport tasks, Sinkhorn DRSB outperforms Wasserstein DRSB under most tested perturbation conditions. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows. • We formulate Distributionally Robust Schrödinger Bridge problems to address initial distri-bution shift. • We derive an exact variational formulation of DRSB and connect its fixed-terminal-cost subproblem to SOC and DRO. • We develop block update algorithms for the Wasserstein and Sinkhorn variants using SOC optimality conditions.

## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 ROBUSTNESS AGAINST NOISY AND SHIFTED DISTRIBUTIONS ON 2D TRANSPORT TASKS 

We demonstrate the robustness of our DRSB algorithm on the 2D Gaussian-to-Gaussian and GMM-to-GMM transport tasks. We use the sliced Wasserstein distance (SWD) between the terminal and target distributions as our metric. We use a standard SB model trained with the Diffusion Schrödinger Bridge Matching (DSBM) algorithm (Shi et al., 2023) as the baseline. We test each trained model against three perturbation types, Gaussian noise (Noisy), directional shift (Shifted), and the DRSB worst-case distribution (Worst), to the initial distribution at test time.

4.2 COMPARISON BETWEEN WASSERSTEIN AND SINKHORN DRSB We compare the two DRSB variants across perturbation types (Table 2 and Figure 2). DRSB-S achieves lower mean SWD in eight of the ten reported conditions. These results indicate greater robustness of DRSB-S under most of the tested perturbation conditions. Figure 2 shows the computed worst case samples for the GMM task. DRSB-S samples are more dispersed due to the entropy regularization, whereas DRSB-W samples are more concentrated. Computation cost. On a single NVIDIA RTX 4090, the additional DRSB stage takes 404 s on Gaussian transport and 1.4 h on FFHQ, after DSBM pretraining of 754 s and 8.5 h. Total training costs are therefore approximately 1.54 × and 1.16 × DSBM. Network gradient updates dominate runtime, accounting for about 80% on Gaussian transport and over 90% on FFHQ. Worst-case optimization accounts for less than 1.5% in the measured runs. DRSB-W uses up to 90 inner gradient steps, whereas DRSB-S samples a Gaussian adversary using the closed-form dual variable of the linearized problem. Appendix I.1 reports the training configurations and stage-wise costs. 

Empirical convergence. We track the variational objective and update diagnostics over 50 epochs on 2D Gaussian transport with ρ = 10 and ϵ = 12 . The objective stabilizes near 312, with a final value of 311.37. At all reported epochs k ≥ 25 , the normalized ambiguity-constraint residual is below 0.005. The final dual variable is 3.483, close to the value 3.496 predicted by the active-constraint SOC relation. The terminal critic mean and mean squared control magnitude also stabilize, ending at 1.550 and 24.30. A separate 120-iteration run at constant learning rate 10 −7 also settles near 312, supporting stabilization beyond the decaying learning-rate schedule. These observations do not establish stationarity of all three blocks or convergence to a saddle point (Appendix I.2). 

SOC optimality condition. The adversarial updates use the relation ∇X0 V (u, X 0; g) = −u0(X0)

from Equation (17). To assess its accuracy for the trained controller, we compute the cost gradient by discrete adjoint recursion and compare it with −uθ (0 , X 0) on the same Gaussian task. Across the five evaluated epochs, their direction cosines are 0.9996–0.9998 and gradient-to-control magnitude ratios are 0.9310–1.0761. At epoch 50, these values are 0.9998 and 1.0089. Thus, both direction and magnitude approximately satisfy the SOC relation on the evaluated samples, supporting its use in the adversarial update for this experiment. This is an empirical consistency check, not a proof of exact SOC optimality (Appendix I.3). 95 RELATED WORK 

Distributionally Robust Optimization. Beginning with the work of Scarf et al. (1957), early DRO focused on moment-based ambiguity sets (Ghaoui et al., 2003; Delage & Ye, 2010). Because moments have limited expressive power, the DRO literature shifted toward distance-based ambiguity sets. Wasserstein DRO (Mohajerin Esfahani & Kuhn, 2018; Zhao & Guan, 2018; Blanchet & Murthy, 2019; Gao & Kleywegt, 2023; Zhang et al., 2025) has attracted attention because of the geometric interpretation of the Wasserstein distance. KL divergence offers convexity and a tractable inner maximization (Ben-Tal et al., 2013; Hu & Hong, 2013). However, its absolute continuity requirement and abstract interpretation are limitations. Recently, Sinkhorn DRO has been proposed to improve computational tractability while modeling realistic uncertainty (Wang et al., 2025). 

Robust Optimal Transport. The OT literature studies how to reduce the sensitivity of transport maps to outliers. One approach relaxes the marginal constraints in OT (Mukherjee et al., 2021). In particular, unbalanced OT (UOT) formulations are used for this purpose (Balaji et al., 2020; Fatras et al., 2021; Choi et al., 2023). More closely related to our approach, the soft-constrained Schrödinger Bridge (Garg et al., 2024) relaxes terminal boundary constraints to prevent overfitting to noisy training samples. Wang et al. (2024) use a UOT distance within a DRO framework to handle outliers in training data. These methods and DRSB address different forms of robustness. DRSB focuses on distribution shifts at test time. 

## 6 CONCLUSION AND LIMITATIONS 

We formulate and solve the DRSB problem, which aims to transport noisy or shifted input distributions to the target distribution. We focus on the Wasserstein and Sinkhorn variants of DRSB. A globally solved Wasserstein inner problem bounds the fixed-terminal-cost objective, while the linearized Sinkhorn inner problem permits Gaussian sampling. Exploring other distances for DRSB is a direction for future work. Minimax adversarial training can be unstable in high-dimensional settings and requires careful hyperparameter tuning and initialization. The additional experiments quantify noise robustness and training overhead. The convergence diagnostics are empirical and do not establish convergence of the full algorithm. 10 REFERENCES

All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. The drift network uθ and the terminal cost network gϕ are both parameterized as multi-layer perceptrons. For the 2D transport tasks (Gaussian and GMM), the baseline SB model is pretrained with the DSBM algorithm (Shi et al., 2023), and the DRSB training is initialized from this pretrained model following the successive approximation scheme in Algorithm 1. For the FFHQ image translation task, the model operates in the 512-dimensional latent space of the ALAE autoencoder (Pidhorskyi et al., 2020). F.1 PRACTICAL TECHNIQUES FOR ADVERSARIAL TRAINING 

The minimax structure of DRSB can make training unstable, particularly in high-dimensional settings. The following techniques stabilize the adversarial optimization. 

Gradual adversarial training. The adversary in DRSB is the worst-case optimization, and its penalty is controlled by the dual variable λ. Gradually decreasing λ from a large initial value increases the adversarial shift slowly. In DRSB-W, this occurs naturally through the λ gradient descent steps (Figure 4a). In DRSB-S, the closed-form λ∗ can produce extreme worst-case shifts from the start. To mitigate this, we linearly increase the ambiguity set radius ε over the first few iterations (Figure 4b).

H.1 WORST -C ASE COST INTERPRETATION 

Remark H.1 (Fixed-terminal-cost bound) . Fix a controller u and terminal cost g. If ˜μ globally maximizes EX0∼μ[V (u, X 0; g)] over Mϵ(ˆ μ), then 

∀μ ∈ Mϵ(ˆ μ), EX0∼μ[V (u, X 0; g)] ≤ EX0∼˜μ[V (u, X 0; g)] . (137) This is a direct consequence of global inner optimality. It does not require an optimal controller or imply that the ambiguity constraint is active. Approximate adversarial updates need not attain this bound. Since g is fixed, the inequality is not a certificate for the full objective with terminal KL divergence. Applying this bound to a test distribution requires verifying its membership in the ambiguity set. For empirical training and test distributions, the Wasserstein distance is well defined and can be computed from their samples. With the squared Euclidean transport cost, the Wasserstein ambiguity set constrains the average squared displacement from the nominal distribution. H.2 EMPIRICAL COST COMPARISON
