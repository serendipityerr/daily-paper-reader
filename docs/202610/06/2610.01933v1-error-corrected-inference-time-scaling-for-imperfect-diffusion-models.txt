## ABSTRACT 

Inference-time scaling adapts pretrained diffusion models to new sampling tasks without additional training. Existing methods rely primarily on Monte Carlo sam-pling with more particles, yet are premised on the pretrained model being exact. In practice, data and training limitations make the model imperfect, and these meth-ods inherit its error. More particles reduce Monte Carlo error but cannot remove the mismatch between the endpoint and the desired target or the error in tracking the prescribed probability path. We introduce the Energy-based Feynman–Kac Corrector (EBFKC) , a framework for energy-based diffusion models that corrects these errors on the fly given a reference energy. We first derive Feynman–Kac dynamics that track a prescribed path exactly in the continuous-time population limit even when the model is imperfect, and approximate these dynamics using sequential Monte Carlo with variance-controlling guidance. To remove the end-point mismatch, we use the pretrained energy as a surrogate along the diffusion path and progressively incorporate the discrepancy between the learned and target terminal energies. Experiments on Gaussian mixture models, particle systems, alanine dipeptide, and alanine tetrapeptide show that our method closely matches target distributions and molecular free-energy profiles under annealing and reward tilting, whereas standard inference-time scaling baselines retain substantial sam-pling errors. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Diffusion models (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021) have become a lead-ing paradigm in generative modeling, achieving remarkable performance across high-dimensional applications ranging from visual synthesis (Peebles & Xie, 2023; Rombach et al., 2022; Karras et al., 2022) to scientific discovery (Watson et al., 2023; Duan et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2022). By learning a time-dependent score function, a diffusion model defines a transport from a tractable prior distri-bution to a complex target distribution. However, training such models is expensive, particularly as modern datasets and architectures continue to grow. It is therefore often impractical to train a separate diffusion model for every new target distribution of interest. One natural strategy is to perform additional training after pretraining, commonly referred to as fine-tuning (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2025; Han et al., 2025; Uehara et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2026). However, fine-tuning remains computationally expensive. This limitation has motivated inference-time scaling, which adapts a pretrained diffusion model to a new sampling task by allocating addi-tional computation at inference time. One class of existing approaches consists of guidance-based methods, which modify the reverse dynamics using classifier guidance (Dhariwal & Nichol, 2021), classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2021), or many variants (He et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2024; Li et al., 2026a). More recently, probabilistic inference methods have gained attention as a princi-pled approach to inference-time scaling by casting the problem within Bayesian inference and Monte Carlo sampling frameworks (Chung et al., 2022; Bruna & Han, 2024), with particular emphasis on Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) methods (Wu et al., 2023; Ren et al., 2026b; Wang et al., 2026). In parallel, energy-based diffusion models have been developed (Yu et al., 2026; OuYang et al., 2026;

Despite their theoretical grounding, these methods commonly assume the pretrained diffusion model is perfect (Ren et al., 2026b; He et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026). In practice, finite data, limited capac-ity, and imperfect optimization make the learned score only approximate, so the model’s marginals deviate from the true ones. Because existing methods treat these approximate marginals as perfect, they inherit this error: adding particles reduces Monte Carlo error but cannot remove the pretrained model error itself. The error is especially pronounced in out-of-distribution regions, where the learned score is less reliable yet the model is often deployed (Lewis et al., 2025). Tellingly, Soif-fer et al. (2026) show that in this regime it is score estimation error, not the vanilla inference-time approximation error, that dominates. Inference-time reweighting derived under an exact-model as-sumption can therefore leave substantial pretrained model error uncorrected, motivating our goal of correcting this error. In this work, we introduce error-corrected inference-time scaling, realized by the Energy-based Feynman–Kac Corrector (EBFKC) , a framework that corrects pretrained model error on the fly given a reference energy. The reference energy specifies which distribution to sample but not how to explore it: on high-dimensional, multimodal targets, Monte Carlo sampling from the energy alone mixes slowly across modes, whereas a pretrained diffusion model already encodes a learned trans-port that reaches them efficiently. We first derive the full Feynman–Kac reweighting potential for any prescribed measure transport path, without assuming that the underlying score satisfies the base diffusion equation. We approximate the resulting dynamics with SMC and use variance-controlling guidance to mitigate particle-weight degeneracy. Then we construct an energy-aligned path that uses a pretrained energy-based diffusion model as a surrogate along the diffusion path while pro-gressively correcting the discrepancy between the learned and target energies to remove the endpoint mismatch. To our knowledge, EBFKC is the first inference-time scaling method that corrects the path-tracking error of an imperfect pretrained model and, treating the pretrained energy as an ap-proximate surrogate, provably realigns the terminal distribution to the target energy, rather than composing or reweighting several assumed-exact models. 

Our Contributions. Building on this insight, our work makes the following contributions: • Energy-based Feynman–Kac Corrector (EBFKC). We derive the Feynman–Kac–type Fokker– Planck equation for a general prescribed probability path, freeing it from the model’s own marginals, and adapt the Variance-Controlling SMC (Ren et al., 2026b) to track it while controlling particle-weight variance. Unlike the Feynman–Kac Corrector (Skreta et al., 2025), EBFKC tracks the path exactly at the population level even when the pretrained model is imperfect. • Progressive target-energy correction. Instantiating this freedom, we keep the imperfect pre-trained energy as a surrogate proposal while using the reference energy to pin the terminal distribu-tion to the desired energy-aligned target (EAT), removing the endpoint mismatch that increasing the number of particles alone cannot eliminate. We call the resulting method EBFKC on EAT .• Error correction across benchmarks. Across Gaussian mixture models, particle systems, ala-nine dipeptide, and alanine tetrapeptide, we show that a moderately imperfect pretrained model can severely bias existing inference-time scaling methods, and that EBFKC on EAT corrects this bias at inference time, recovering target distributions and molecular free-energy profiles where these baselines fail.

Our method can be viewed as annealed SMC along a diffusion-based probability path. Traditional annealed SMC typically uses a geometric tempering path between the initial and target densities. Its intermediate densities can be evaluated up to normalization, but transporting particles between separated modes can remain difficult (M´ at´ e & Fleuret, 2023). We describe the geometric tempering SMC baseline in App. B.6 and compare it with EBFKC on EAT in Lennard-Jones systems (Sec. 4.2). Existing work replaces the tempering path with a diffusion path, whose progressive Gaussian smoothing eases transport between modes, and uses it to build Boltzmann Generators, i.e., sam-plers for p⋆(x) ∝ exp( −E⋆(x)) with E⋆ given (Phillips et al., 2024; Grenioux & Noble, 2026). These methods use a reweighting strategy similar to that in Thm. 2, specialized to Φt = Eθ (·, t ),where the architecture of Eθ enforces Eθ (·, T ) = E⋆ by construction. We generalize this to an arbitrary reference energy, sampling from qref  

> T

rather than from p⋆ alone, and without requiring the terminal constraint to be baked into the architecture. This perspective also motivates our choice of building energy-based rather than score-based diffu-sion models. Reweighting requires evaluating the density along the path, which an energy model provides directly. With only a score, the density must be recovered by integrating the score for every particle at every step, which is costly and adds numerical error (Sj¨ oberg et al., 2026; Lam et al., 2026), making efficient and reliable reweighting impractical. 

## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

We evaluate whether EBFKC on EAT recovers the reference target despite pretrained model error, on annealing and reward-tilting tasks for a high-dimensional Gaussian mixture model, particle systems, alanine dipeptide, and alanine tetrapeptide. We take Eref = E⋆, the scientific setting that motivates our method, for which reference samples are available independently of the pretrained model. We compare against two baselines that each remove only one failure mode: (1) EBFKC on MIT tracks its path correctly but terminates at the MIT, isolating the endpoint mismatch; (2) FKC on EAT runs FKC with the reward redefined as ˆr = r − γ∆Eref , which moves its endpoint to q⋆T (App. B.5), isolating the path-tracking error. Across all systems, only EBFKC on EAT recovers the reference, showing that both failure modes arise in practice and must be addressed. To quantify the computational cost of these accuracy gains, we compare runtimes at the reported sampling budgets. EBFKC on EAT takes approximately 1.1–3.6× the runtime of EBFKC on MIT in these comparisons, owing to additional energy and derivative evaluations (App. D.1–D.5). Unless marked “w/o VCG”, all methods use VCG (Ren et al., 2026b), which we found essential for stable weight dynamics in both FKC and EBFKC. ## 5 CONCLUSION 

We introduced the Energy-based Feynman–Kac Corrector (EBFKC) , a framework for inference-time scaling with imperfect pretrained energy-based diffusion models given a reference energy. EBFKC derives Feynman–Kac dynamics that track any prescribed path exactly at the population 9Preprint level and simulates them with SMC, using VCG to mitigate weight degeneracy, thereby removing the path-tracking error. The energy-aligned target (EAT) uses the pretrained energy as a surrogate along the path and progressively incorporates the reference energy, removing the endpoint mis-match. Across Gaussian mixture models, particle systems, and molecular systems, both ingredients prove necessary, and EBFKC on EAT recovers the target distributions under annealing and reward tilting and yields accurate molecular free-energy profiles. Future directions include extending the EBFKC framework to discrete diffusion models (Hasan et al., 2026; Ren et al., 2026a), adapting the correction schedules automatically, which we currently design by hand for the high-barrier alanine tetrapeptide system, reducing derivative-evaluation costs, and correcting large pretrained equilib-rium samplers for more reliable inference-time scaling in challenging real-world applications (Lewis et al., 2025). The EAT path evaluates the reference and learned terminal energies, while both MIT and EAT paths evaluate the reward, at the current state xt. At high noise levels, these states can lie far from the data manifold, where evaluating E⋆ and its gradient can be numerically unstable. Therefore, in the challenging LJ-13 and molecular sampling tasks we evaluate these quantities at a look-ahead point defined by the map 

Dt (x) = (1 − ωt) x + ωt Dt(x), (16) which interpolates between x and the denoiser prediction Dt(x) of Eq. Two schedules govern the correction: the look-ahead strength ωt and the correction schedule κt. Since the denoiser is weakly informative early in the reverse process, we activate the correction only later; over its active window ωt decreases from the denoiser toward the identity, and ωT = 0 gives DT (x) = x.
