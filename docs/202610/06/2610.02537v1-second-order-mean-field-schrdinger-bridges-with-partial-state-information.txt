Second-Order Mean-Field Schr¨odinger Bridges
with Partial State Information
Asmaa Eldesoukey, Md Zulfiqur Haider, Italo Napolitano, Yongxin Chen, and Abhishek Halder
Abstract— Motivated by scenarios in control systems where
decision making is informed by only partial information about
the states, we introduce a novel formulation of the Schr¨odinger
bridge problem for inertial interacting-agent systems. Within
our setting, the agent ensemble is steered to match partial
specifications of the collective configuration. Our analysis
pertains to a mean-field framework in which the collective
dynamics are described by a nonlinear Vlasov–Fokker–Planck
integro-PDE with nonlocal interactions. We assume that only
distributions over positions are specified at the endpoints
of a finite time horizon, representing aggregate population
snapshots, while the corresponding velocity degrees of freedom
remain unspecified. We derive the associated optimality system,
identifying the optimal controlled evolution as jointly informed
by the initial and terminal specifications through forward and
backward propagation in time. We also show that the partial
endpoint specifications bring about distinct conditions on the
optimal control and the consistent evolution. Subsequently, to
compute solutions to the optimality system, we develop a nested
fixed-point algorithm inspired by the classical Fortet–Sinkhorn
iteration, but extended to handle the nonlinearities arising from
nonlocal interactions and temporal coupling.
I. INTRODUCTION
Interacting-agent systems are common in a broad spec-
trum of control engineering applications, ranging from au-
tonomous swarms [1], [2] to cyber-physical infrastructures
[3] and to synthetic active matter [4]. In such complex sys-
tems, emergent macroscopic phenomena arise from pairwise
interactions among constituent agents [5]. Indeed, interaction
mechanisms can effect desired collective behaviors, yet they
may not align with finite-horizon control objectives. For
instance, repulsive interactions promote collision avoidance
in robotic swarms; however, they can hinder the control
requirement to assemble within a target region. Thus, a
natural control problem is to steer an agent ensemble toward
a desired aggregate configuration while accounting for the
interaction mechanisms integral to the collective dynamics.
The aim of this work is to design collective control policies
guided by partial, rather than full statistical knowledge of
system states. Such a perspective is motivated by scenarios
in which comprehensive information about interacting popu-
lations, especially those of large scale, may not be available,

horizon steering of interacting inertial agents subject to
stochastic disturbances, in which only the spatial probability
distributions are specified at the initial and terminal times.
The corresponding velocity distributions are left unspecified

We focus on dynamics in the mean-field limit, where the
system evolves according to a nonlinear Vlasov–Fokker–
Planck equation with nonlocal interactions [6].
Our formulation is rooted in the theory of Schr¨odinger
bridges that traces back to E. Schr¨odinger’s articles [7], [8].
Concerned with particle systems, the classical Schr¨odinger
bridge problem seeks the most likely paths that the particles
traverse between given initial and final probability distribu-
tions. From a stochastic control standpoint, the Schr¨odinger
bridge identifies this intermediate stochastic evolution via a
minimum-energy update of the underlying system dynamics
[9]. Specifically, the update is an added drift vector field that
is viewed as the control input required to steer the particles
between the specified marginals.
Related works. The Schr¨odinger bridge problem with
spatial endpoint constraints for non-interacting second-
order dynamics has been studied in [10], [11]. Mean-field
Schr¨odinger bridges have also been considered for inter-
acting populations with first-order dynamics (i.e., kinemat-
ics) in [12]. Numerical methods for addressing these first-
order mean-field bridge problems have been developed in
[13], [14]. Minimum-energy control for finite collections of
second-order stochastic oscillators has been explored in [15],
but the mean-field limit formulation has not been considered.
For broad classes of stochastic interacting systems, the works
[16], [17] showed, with different degrees of generality, that
the required cost to identify the most likely evolution is
a relative entropy functional and provided the equivalent
stochastic control cost.
However, those works did not address the endpoint-
constrained Schr¨odinger bridge problem for interacting ki-
netic systems, a direction the present authors recently initi-
ated for Cucker–Smale and Morse swarming models in [18].
The present work builds on this, extending the formulation
to systems under general interactions.

solutions. We explain how the resulting optimal controller

VI. CONCLUDING REMARKS
This work has relied on Schr¨odinger’s dictum of bridges to
synthesize suitable collective control policies when sensing
of an interacting ensemble is limited to partial, aggregate
information. Specifically, we considered assimilating spatial
snapshot data and inertial mean-field dynamics into the
bridge formulation. We have identified the structure of the
optimal policy and the resulting controlled evolution and
proposed a nested fixed-point approach to compute the
corresponding optimality system.
Future research directions include uncertainty in interac-
tion mechanisms. Another direction is to extend the present
framework to entail time-resolved distributions (a multi-
marginal formulation). This is particularly relevant to scenar-
ios where time series of population snapshots are available,
such as in trajectory inference [31], [32].
