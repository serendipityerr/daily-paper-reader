# BRIDGE TWIST : Twisting Schrödinger Bridges for Training-Free Conditional Sampling 

Mengyu Li, Qianqian Qu, Jun S. Liu ∗

## Abstract 

Generative models are often required to serve many downstream conditional tasks. In the training-free setting, a pretrained unconditional model is reused as a prior and combined with a task-specific likelihood to sample from the corresponding poste-rior. While such training-free samplers have been developed for diffusion and flow models, their counterpart for Schrödinger bridge (SB) models remains unexplored, despite the growing appeal of SBs as flexible stochastic transport models between general source and target distributions. We propose B RIDGE TWIST , a training-free conditional sampler for pretrained SB generative models, formulated as a tempered twisted sequential Monte Carlo scheme. Its twist is a closed-form plug-in surrogate, obtained by combining the velocity and score fields exposed by SB pretraining into an endpoint predictor and evaluating the likelihood there. Since this surrogate is less reliable near the source, we temper the twist with a time-varying exponent, downweighting its influence at early steps. We further introduce a bridge-shaped inference-time diffusion schedule that concentrates the proposal noise in the inte-rior of the path. Telescoping importance weights then cancel all intermediate twist factors, yielding an asymptotically exact sampler for the posterior. Empirically, BRIDGE TWIST outperforms training-free baselines on class-conditional and text-prompted image generation, and is competitive with specialized inverse problem solvers on inpainting, deblurring, and super-resolution. 

## 1 Introduction 

Conditional sampling draws from a generative model subject to specific constraints. In the training-free regime, a pretrained unconditional model is treated as a prior pθ (x) ≈ pdata (x) and combined with a tractable likelihood Ly (x) = p(y|x). Bayes’ rule then defines the target posterior pθ (x|y) ∝

pθ (x)Ly (x), allowing a single pretrained model to support many downstream conditional tasks without retraining. Different choices of Ly recover classifier-guided sampling, inverse problems, text-guided generation, and protein motif scaffolding [1–5]. For score-based diffusion and flow matching priors, training-free conditional sampling has been developed along two main lines. Gradient guidance and projection methods modify the unconditional sampling dynamics with likelihood-driven corrections at each step [2, 6–10]; they are simple and effective, but generally introduce irreducible approximation error. Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) samplers instead propagate a set of particles with reweighting and resampling, yielding consistent posterior samples and often stronger performance in various conditional tasks [11, 1, 12, 3, 13, 14, 5]. Across these training-free methods, an important component is a model-specific endpoint predictor from the current intermediate state; e.g., it is used to form lookahead twists in SMC samplers.

Schrödinger bridge (SB) generative models [15–18] provide a recent alternative to diffusion and flow priors. An SB connects a source X0 ∼ q0 to a target X1 ∼ q1; among all path laws with these source and target marginals, it selects the one that minimizes the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence to a prescribed reference law. This combination of a general source and stochastic transport is useful for image generation [15, 19, 20], image restoration [21, 22], cell trajectory inference [18, 23], and other tasks. Existing SB-based conditional methods [24, 21, 25, 26] train a task-specific network for each likelihood family. Training-free conditional sampling that reuses a pretrained unconditional SB prior remains unexplored. Under a Brownian reference with diffusion scale σ > 0, an SB conditioned on its endpoints (X0, X 1)

is a Brownian bridge. At each t ∈ (0 , 1) , we can therefore write Xt = (1 − t)X0 + tX 1 + εt, where 

εt ∼ N (0 , σ 2t(1 − t)I) is independent of (X0, X 1). For endpoint prediction, diffusion samplers use score-based denoising, and flow samplers use velocity-based extrapolation (Table 1). For SBs, however, score-only denoising estimates the interpolation (1 − t)X0 + tX 1 instead of X1, while velocity-only extrapolation omits a score correction. Our key observation is that the probability-flow velocity v⋆t and marginal score s⋆t are complementary, and combining these two fields gives the endpoint conditional mean: E[X1|Xt = x] = x + (1 − t)v⋆t (x) + ( σ2/2)(1 − t)s⋆t (x) (Prop. 1). Their neural approximations can be obtained through score and flow matching [18] or from learned forward and backward SB drifts [15, 16]. Using these pretrained fields, we propose B RIDGE TWIST , a tempered twisted SMC sampler (Figure 1). The ideal twist, i.e., the predictive likelihood E[Ly (X1)|Xt], is unavailable analytically, and using pilot rollouts to estimate it [27, 28] requires many network evaluations per particle and step. We instead use a plug-in twist Ly ( ˆX1(Xt)) to approximate, where ˆX1(Xt) is the velocity–score endpoint prediction. This twist discards uncertainty in X1 given Xt and can be over-confident near the source. We therefore temper its influence with a time-varying temperature coefficient. In an isotropic linear-Gaussian model, the tempered plug-in twist can recover the exact twist (Remark 1). 2With velocity and score fields, we can vary the diffusion coefficient g(t) at inference time while preserving the SB marginals (Prop. 2) [18, 29]. We use this flexibility to introduce a bridge-shaped schedule g(t) ∝ pt(1 − t), following the time dependence of the Brownian bridge standard deviation. This schedule concentrates noise in the middle of the sampling path. It reduces noise near the source, where guidance is weak, and near the target, where additional noise can disrupt particles already aligned with the conditioning information. 

Our contributions. We believe that the following progresses are novel. • Training-free SB conditioning. BRIDGE TWIST is, to our knowledge, the first training-free condi-tional sampler for pretrained Schrödinger bridges, supporting arbitrary differentiable likelihoods. • Tempered plug-in twist. We construct a lookahead twist from the velocity–score endpoint predictor and temper its influence along the path, without changing the final target conditional distribution. • Noise schedule and consistency. We introduce a bridge-shaped diffusion schedule and importance correction; Theorem 1 bounds the L2 error for K particles by order K−1/2 relative to the learned and discretized posterior. • Plug-and-play for diverse tasks. BRIDGE TWIST achieves leading performance on various image generation and restoration tasks without task-specific retraining. 

## 2 Background 

Notation. Random objects are denoted by uppercase letters (e.g., Xt), and their realizations by lowercase ones (e.g., xt). We write N (μ, Σ) for a Gaussian density and I for the identity matrix. The time index is t ∈ [0 , 1] , and its discretization is tn for n ∈ { 0, . . . , N } with t0 = 0 and tN = 1 .

Twisted SMC [37, 35] mitigates this by inserting intermediate twisting functions, also called lookahead functions. Under the learned prior Pθ,g , the ideal twist is the predictive likelihood 

ht(x) := E[Ly (X1) | Xt = x], t ∈ [0 , 1] . (4) This ideal twist is intractable because it integrates over all future trajectories, and direct Monte Carlo estimation is prohibitively expensive. Existing twisted SMC samplers therefore approximate ht

with model-specific surrogates, using Tweedie’s endpoint mean inside the likelihood for diffusion priors [1], and the Euler one-step endpoint for flow priors [5]. Section 3 develops the counterpart for Schrödinger bridges. 

## 3 Method 

We consider conditioning information y supplied through a differentiable likelihood Ly (x) = p(y|x),possibly known up to a normalizing constant. Conditional sampling aims to draw from the endpoint posterior π⋆(x1) ∝ pθ (x1)Ly (x1). This endpoint posterior is the terminal marginal of the path posterior Π⋆ defined in Section 2.2. Section 3.1 constructs a tractable surrogate for the ideal twist ht(x) = E[Ly (X1)|Xt = x]. Sec-tion 3.2 develops the B RIDGE TWIST sampler by combining temporal tempering, guided proposals, a bridge-shaped diffusion schedule, and importance correction. Section 3.3 bounds the resulting finite-particle approximation error.

Bridge-shaped diffusion schedule. At inference time, g is flexible (Section 2.1). A constant 

g(t) ≡ σ matches the Brownian reference but allocates a uniform proposal variance σ2∆n across 

n. This is mismatched with the conditional bridge variance σ2t(1 − t), which instead vanishes at both endpoints, and the excess proposal noise can increase the variance of importance weights near 

t ∈ { 0, 1}. We therefore introduce a bridge-shaped schedule g(t) = cg σpt(1 − t) + δg , where 

cg > 0 is an inference diffusion scale and the small δg > 0 keeps g positive at t ∈ { 0, 1}.

## 4 Experiments 

We compare B RIDGE TWIST with flow matching posterior sampling (FMPS) [6], training-free targeted flow (TFTF) [5], twisted diffusion sampler (TDS) [1], diffusion posterior sampling [2] adapted to the SB prior (DPS-SB), and terminal importance sampling (IS). MNIST provides a sanity check [40]; CIFAR-10 and 256 × 256 CelebA-HQ provide the main image benchmarks [41, 42]. Pretraining and implementation details are in Appendices B–C; additional results are in Appendix D. The code is publicly available at https://github.com/Mengyu8042/BridgeTwist .

4.1 Toy example 

We first evaluate B RIDGE TWIST on a two-dimensional toy example [5]. The target distribution is the Gaussian mixture q1 = 0 .75 N (( −√2, −√2) , I/ 4) + 0 .25 N (( √2, √2) , I/ 4) , the source is q0 = N (0 , I ), and the Brownian reference has σ = 1 . We condition on y = (0 , 0) through the Gaussian likelihood Ly (x1) = N (y; x1, I/ 4) . In this setting, the target posterior is available analytically as π⋆(x1) = 0 .75 N (x1; ( −√2/2, −√2/2) , I/ 8) + 0 .25 N (x1; ( √2/2, √2/2) , I/ 8) .All velocity and score fields are computed in closed form, so this experiment isolates the conditional sampler from neural network approximation errors (Appendix C.1). We use N = 100 discretization steps and K ∈ { 100 , 200 , 500 , 1000 , 2000 } particles, and report the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) to the analytic posterior over 50 repetitions. The left panel of Figure 2 shows that B RIDGE TWIST achieves the lowest MMD. Its empirical MMD 2

decays approximately as K−1, with a fitted log-log slope of −1.01 . SMC-based methods improve as K increases, with B RIDGE TWIST outperforming both flow-based TFTF and diffusion-based TDS. FMPS and DPS-SB plateau at higher errors because they use approximate guidance without importance weight correction, leaving bias that persists as K increases. The right panel indicates that BRIDGE TWIST closely matches the two posterior modes and their relative mass.

## 5 Discussion 

We introduce B RIDGE TWIST , a training-free conditional sampler that reuses pretrained unconditional Schrödinger bridge models. Central to our approach are three contributions to SB conditioning: a plug-in lookahead built from the velocity–score endpoint predictor, temporal tempering that moderates this lookahead when conditional endpoint uncertainty is large, and a bridge-shaped diffusion schedule that concentrates exploration in the path interior. These designs guide and reweight particles through a sequence of intermediate targets toward the endpoint posterior. We establish a finite-particle error bound for estimates under the posterior, and demonstrate competitive performance across class-conditional and text-prompted image generation, and image restoration. Beyond the sampler itself, the endpoint predictor offers a two-channel view that connects the transport perspective of flow models with the denoising perspective of diffusion models. For an SB under a Brownian reference, the velocity channel describes transport along the bridge, while the score channel corrects for noise in the current state. Our ablations support combining both channels to improve class alignment and sample quality. 

Limitations and future work. First, B RIDGE TWIST currently is restricted to Schrödinger bridges with the Brownian reference, and extending it to other references require reference-specific endpoint predictors. Second, its twist approximation uses only the conditional endpoint mean and can be inaccurate for strongly curved likelihoods or multimodal conditional endpoint laws. The second-order extension in Appendix D.8 improves some low-dimensional cases, but full covariance estimation is expensive in high dimensions and requires scalable approximations. Third, the sampler targets the posterior induced by the learned and discretized prior, leaving prior-learning and discretization errors. Fourth, preparing the SB prior also requires an additional score head, with a one-time computational overhead relative to directly using pretrained flow or diffusion models. Finally, future work also includes protein design and broader single-cell transport applications, building on the initial example in Appendix D.9.

## B Pretraining details 

This appendix provides the SB pretraining convention assumed by the main text. We follow the simulation-free score-and-flow matching (SF2M) framework [18] but with a different parameteriza-tion, detailed below.
