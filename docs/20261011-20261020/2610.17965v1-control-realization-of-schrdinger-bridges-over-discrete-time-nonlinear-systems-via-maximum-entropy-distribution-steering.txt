# Control Realization of Schr ¨ odinger Bridges over Discrete-Time Nonlinear Systems via Maximum-Entropy Distribution Steering 

Kaito Ito and Kenji Kashima 

Abstract — This paper shows how a Schr ¨ odinger bridge (SB) structure emerges from maximum-entropy (MaxEnt) distribution steering of discrete-time nonlinear systems. We also establish that the resulting bridge can be realized by an optimal control policy. We consider general state and input costs, non-Gaussian endpoint distributions, and input constraints. Assuming the existence of a feasible policy with finite objective value, we show that the state-path measure under a MaxEnt optimal control policy is the unique SB. The reference measure of this SB is in-duced by an optimal policy of the corresponding terminal-unconstrained problem. Moreover, we give an equivalent characterization of the optimal state-path measure using a killed reference and its normalized Feynman–Kac measure, thereby interpreting the state cost as killing. We then char-acterize the finite-cost feasibility by the existence of an end-point coupling having finite Kullback–Leibler divergence from the reference endpoint distribution. For compactly supported marginals with bounded target density, we de-rive reachability-based sufficient conditions guaranteeing this feasibility. We further provide specializations to fully actuated control-affine and reachable linear systems. 

Index Terms — Schr ¨ odinger bridge, optimal control, dis-tribution steering, discrete-time nonlinear systems. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

# THIS paper studies discrete-time Schr¨ odinger bridges (SBs) on continuous spaces. The SB problem originates from an inquiry by Schr¨ odinger [1], [2]: given marginals and prior dynamics, what is the most probable trajectory connecting the two prescribed marginals? In a standard modern formulation, it is posed as the minimization of the Kullback– Leibler (KL) divergence between the path measure over tra-jectories and the reference measure induced by the prior [3]. SB problems can be understood as entropy-regularized optimal transport [4] and have recently found applications in generative modeling [5], image restoration [6], system identification from snapshot data [7], and trajectory inference from single-cell data [8]. The SB problem is inherently an optimal distribution steer-ing problem that transports the state distribution from the

initial distribution to the desired distribution, and there is a rich literature on the control-theoretic viewpoint of SBs, especially for continuous-time systems [9]–[12]; see [13] for a comprehensive review and [14] for a monograph on SBs and stochastic control with fixed marginals. Under the distribution control minimizing a cumulative quadratic control cost, the state process follows an SB, where the prior is induced by the uncontrolled dynamics driven by noise. When the cost functional contains state costs, the distribution steering prob-lem is equivalent to an SB problem with killing of probability mass [11], [15]–[17]. Despite extensive studies on the relationships between SBs and control, comparatively limited attention has been devoted to discrete-time settings [18]–[22]. The work [23] established that, for possibly degenerate discrete-time linear systems with Gaussian driving noise and Gaussian endpoint marginals, the SB is equivalent to optimal distribution steering with an entropy-regularized quadratic input cost. Entropy-regularized, or maximum-entropy (MaxEnt), distribution steering with state and input costs was subsequently studied in the linear-quadratic-Gaussian (LQG) setting [24], and an SB character-ization was established under invertibility assumptions on the system and input matrices [25]. Beyond the LQG setting, how-ever, it remains unclear whether and how MaxEnt distribution steering can be characterized as an SB for nonlinear dynamics, non-Gaussian endpoint marginals, and general state and input costs. This paper addresses these questions. 

Contributions: The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: 1) We establish the equivalence between MaxEnt distri-bution steering of deterministic discrete-time nonlinear systems and an SB, allowing non-Gaussian marginals, general state and input costs, and input constraints (Theorem 1). The SB reference is induced by the optimal state process of the corresponding terminal-unconstrained control problem. We also characterize optimal Markov policies that realize the bridge (Propo-sitions 4–6). Moreover, we interpret the state cost as a killing mechanism by showing that the optimal state-path measure is also the unique minimizer of the SB problems defined by the killed reference and its normal-ized Feynman–Kac reference (Theorem 2). 2) We prove that finite-cost feasibility of MaxEnt distribu-tion steering is equivalent to the existence of an endpoint coupling with finite KL divergence from the reference arXiv:2610.17965v1 [math.OC] 13 Oct 2026 2

endpoint distribution (Corollary 1). This condition, to-gether with Theorem 1, also guarantees the existence of an optimal control policy. For compactly supported marginals and a bounded target density, we derive reachability-based sufficient conditions for the finite-cost feasibility (Theorem 3). We further provide blockwise, fully actuated control-affine, and linear-system special-izations (Corollaries 2–4). These results reveal a discrete-time SB structure that emerges directly from the control problem. Because our con-struction starts from a Gibbs reference on the control input, it accommodates general deterministic nonlinear dynamics, as well as general input costs and input constraints. This contrasts with the Girsanov-based continuous-time control interpretation of SBs, which relies on control-affine drift perturbations. In that setting, the path-space KL divergence induces a quadratic input cost [9], [11]. Although our SB reference is derived from the associated MaxEnt control problem, its induced path measure retains the noise-driven structure familiar from continuous-time SBs. This construction of the SB reference provides both an admissible control realization of the bridge and access to efficient computational methods for SBs includ-ing the Sinkhorn algorithm (see Subsection III-D). 

Related work: An early study [18] considered discrete-time SBs for Markov processes with transition densities and showed that the SB is again Markov. The Gaussian case was further studied in [19]. Subsequent works studied optimiza-tion of driving-noise distributions under prescribed endpoint state distributions [20], and finite-state SB formulations for network transport [21]. More recently, [22] considered SBs with LQR-type path costs and Gaussian marginals. However, these discrete-time SB formulations do not in general establish an equivalence with optimal control for prior dynamics or provide an admissible control policy that realizes the SB. Our formulation addresses this gap by deriving the SB reference directly from the MaxEnt control problem, which yields both the equivalence and an admissible control realization. In continuous time, state costs have been related to killed reference processes through Feynman–Kac weighting since at least [11]. Our reference construction reveals the correspond-ing discrete-time structure: the state cost appears as a survival weighting of the noise-driven reference, while the resulting normalized Feynman–Kac measure yields the same SB. For distribution steering without entropy regularization, suit-able point-to-point reachability, together with standard com-pactness and continuity assumptions, guarantees feasibility under stochastic feedback [26]. For finite-cost SBs, mere reachability is insufficient. At least one endpoint coupling must also have finite KL divergence with respect to the reference. Our sufficient conditions ensure this by requiring a uniformly nonvanishing family of regular steering inputs that remains in the interior of the admissible input set. 

Organization: The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section II, we formulate the SB and MaxEnt distribution steering problems. In Section III, we establish the SB characterization of the MaxEnt distribution steering and characterize optimal policies. In Section IV, we give an inter-pretation of the state cost through a killed reference measure and a normalized Feynman–Kac measure. In Section V, we derive reachability-based sufficient conditions for the existence of finite-KL endpoint couplings. Section VI concludes the paper.

B. Schr ¨ odinger bridge 

In an SB problem, a reference measure over trajectories and marginal distributions at the endpoints of a time interval are given. The reference measure describes a prior stochas-tic evolution, which was originally modeled as Brownian motion [1], [2] and has been extended to general diffusion processes [9]–[11]. Given prior dynamics, the SB problem seeks a probability distribution over trajectories that satisfies the prescribed endpoint marginals while remaining as close as possible to the reference evolution. Specifically, the discrete-time SB problem is formulated as the minimization of the KL divergence between this trajectory distribution and the refer-ence measure. A probability measure on the state-trajectory space (Rn)N +1 is referred to as a path measure.

VI. C ONCLUSION 

We established an SB characterization of MaxEnt distribu-tion steering for discrete-time nonlinear systems with general state and input costs. By reformulating the control objective as a KL divergence on the state-input space, we showed that the optimal controlled state process is an SB and that the bridge can be realized by a Markov control policy. We also interpreted the state cost through killing and Feynman–Kac weighting, and characterized finite-cost feasibility in terms of a finite-KL endpoint coupling. We further derived reachability-based sufficient conditions for this feasibility, together with specializations to fully actuated control-affine and linear sys-tems. A nonlinear numerical example illustrated the resulting SB construction. Future work includes developing scalable numerical meth-ods for nonlinear and high-dimensional systems, where direct discretization of the state space becomes computationally prohibitive. It is also of interest to derive less conservative and more readily verifiable conditions for finite-cost feasibility.
