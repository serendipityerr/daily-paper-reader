# Optimal transportation under McKean–Vlasov dynamics 

# Camilo Hernández ∗ Ludovic Tangpi †

# October 14, 2026 

Abstract 

We study a broad class of stochastic transport problems under controlled McKean–Vlasov dynamics, allowing control of both drift and volatility, with weak terminal constraints. In contrast to the classical Schrödinger problem, the reference dynamics depend on the evolving law of the process, leading to nonlinear optimization problems on path space, while the terminal constraint is formulated through weak optimal transport, permitting a flexible treatment of distributional targets. This framework encompasses several problems of current interest, including the mean field Schrödinger problem, martingale optimal transport, and Schrödinger–Bass bridges. We establish three main results. First, we prove the existence of optimal controls under general coercivity, continuity, and convexity assumptions. Second, we derive a convex dual representation that extends weak Kantorovich duality and stochastic optimal transport duality to controlled McKean–Vlasov dynamics. Finally, for the mean field Schrödinger problem, we prove convergence, at the level of value functions, of the associated finite-particle Schrödinger bridge problems to the mean field limit, thereby identifying the nonlinear problem as the exact infinite-particle limit of finite-particle Schrödinger bridges. As a byproduct, we establish the existence of an optimal mean field Schrödinger potential, providing a variational foundation for duality in nonlinear stochastic transport. 

Keywords : Mean-field Schrödinger problem; McKean–Vlasov control; Weak optimal transport; Schrödinger bridge; propagation of chaos; interacting particle systems; convex duality.

# 1 Introduction 

A broad class of stochastic transportation problems seeks to interpolate between prescribed probability distributions through the law of a stochastic process, while minimizing a cost associated with its dynamics; see, for instance, Tan and Touzi [50] and Liu and Neufeld [39]. A fundamental example is the Schrödinger problem, which asks for the most likely evolution of a random system conditionally on the observation of its initial and final laws. In its modern formulation, given a reference path measure R on the path space C([0 , T ]; Rm) and two probability measures ν0, ν T , one minimizes the relative entropy H(P | R ) among path measures

Originating in Schrödinger’s work [46; 47] and developed through the theory of reciprocal processes and entropy minimization, this problem has become a central object at the intersection of probability, stochastic analysis, and optimal transport; see, for instance, Föllmer [22], Föllmer and Gantert [23], and Léonard [37, 38]. In this work, we study stochastic transportation problems in which the dynamics of the transported process, as well as the associated transportation cost, may depend on its evolving path measure. This law dependence introduces a genuinely nonlinear feature: the transport mechanism is itself determined by the law of the process being optimized. A prominent instance of this structure is the mean field Schrödinger problem introduced by Backhoff–Veraguas, Conforti, Gentil, and Léonard [2] and further analyzed by Hernández and Tangpi [28]. There, the fixed reference diffusion of the classical Schrödinger problem is replaced by McKean-Vlasov dynamics whose drift depends on the marginal flow of the candidate path measure, so that the reference measure against which relative entropy is computed varies with the path measure being optimized. Mean-field Schrödinger bridge formulations have also been used in computational studies of crowd navigation and opinion dynamics; see Liu, Chen, So, and Theodorou [40] and Inoue, Kalise, and Laurière [30]. We consider such law-dependent transportation problems subject to terminal constraints formulated via weak optimal transport, thereby accommodating distributional targets beyond equality of marginals. An additional difficulty, which will play an important role in our duality analysis, is that the natural McKean-Vlasov formulation lacks the convexity in the terminal distribution that is present in classical stochastic transport theory. We now describe this problem more precisely, while leaving its rigorous formulation to the main body of the paper. 2Contributions and related literature. The main contributions of this paper are threefold. First, we prove the existence of optimizers for a broad class of weak McKean-Vlasov transportation problems, allowing for control of both the drift and volatility. Second, we establish a convex dual formulation extending weak Kantorovich duality and stochastic optimal transport duality to this nonlinear controlled setting. Third, for the mean field Schrödinger problem described above, we show that the problem arises as the limit of finite-particle Schrödinger problems with weakly interacting particles. As a byproduct of the finite-particle analysis, we also establish the existence of an optimal Schrödinger potential for the limiting mean field problem. The first contribution, Theorem 2.5, establishes the existence of optimizers for the weak McKean-Vlasov transportation problem with value Vc(ν0, ν T ). This extends the theory of mean field Schrödinger problems developed in [2; 28] in two directions: the characteristics of the state process may themselves be controlled, including its quadratic variation, and the terminal constraint is relaxed to the weak transport sense. Under lower semicontinuity and convexity assumptions on the weak cost c, coercivity of the running cost in the controlled characteristics, and continuity and linear growth assumptions on the McKean-Vlasov drift, we show that the infimum is attained whenever the value is finite. A notable feature of the result is that it is obtained in a weak formulation and requires no global Lipschitz condition on the drift. The proof relies on the compactness of the controlled laws and the stability of the associated martingale problems, together with the lower semicontinuity of the running cost and of the weak transport value function. The second contribution, Theorem 2.7, is a convex dual representation of the weak McKean-Vlasov transportation problem. The result admits a natural geometric interpretation. The value, viewed as a function of its terminal marginal, is convex and lower semicontinuous and is therefore determined by its supporting hyperplanes. Our duality theorem identifies these hyperplanes explicitly in terms of weak Kantorovich potentials φ, while the weak c-transform

Thus, the dual potentials act as Lagrange multipliers for the terminal weak transport relation. The formula relies on the weak transport duality of Gozlan, Roberto, Samson, and Tetali [25] and Backhoff-Veraguas, Beiglböck, and Pammer [3] and extends the stochastic optimal transport duality developed by Mikami and Thieullen [42] and subsequently in [50]. A distinctive feature of our formulation is the use of exogenous randomization in the McKean-Vlasov law flow. This is essential for convex duality: as we show by an explicit example in Section 3.2, the value function associated with deterministic law flows need not be convex in the terminal distribution. Allowing randomized flows restores this convexity in the terminal distribution, and Theorem 2.7 further shows that the resulting value is precisely the lower-semicontinuous convex envelope of the deterministic-flow value. In contrast with the latter setting, both the dynamics and the costs may depend on the law of the controlled process, while the terminal constraint is imposed in the sense of weak optimal transport. The third contribution, Theorem 2.12, is a finite-particle approximation theorem for the mean field Schrödinger problem 

Ve(ν0, ν T ). We consider N weakly interacting controlled diffusions, started from an i.i.d. configuration, whose drifts interact through the empirical measure and whose terminal marginal constraints are imposed particle by particle. See Assumption C for detailed structural assumptions. If VNe (ν0, ν T ) denotes the corresponding N -particle cost, we prove the convergence of the cost per particle, 1

N VNe (ν0, ν T ) −→ V e(ν0, ν T ).

The theorem thus identifies the nonlinear mean field Schrödinger problem as the exact infinite-particle limit of finite-particle Schrödinger problems. It can be viewed as a propagation of chaos result at the level of constrained variational problems, rather than solely at the level of the underlying dynamics. A central difficulty is that the terminal constraint induces strong dependence among the particles at time T , while their controls are themselves coupled through the empirical measure. To the best of our knowledge, our analysis also provides the first self-contained proof of the existence of an optimal Schrödinger potential for the mean field Schrödinger problem; see Theorem 2.13. That is, there is a function φ ∈ L2(Rm, ν T ) with a νT -square-integrable weak gradient (in the sense of distributions) such that 

Ve(ν0, ν T ) = Ψ φ(ν0) − ⟨ φ, ν T ⟩.

In comparison, [2, Section 1.3.2] discusses the existence of such a potential under additional assumptions on the so-called “corrector” associated with the optimal mean field Schrödinger bridge. The proof of Theorem 2.12 relies essentially on the convex duality developed above and proceeds through matching lower and upper bounds. The lower bound follows from compactness and entropy estimates, together with mean field limit results for controlled McKean-Vlasov systems from Djete, Possamaï, and Tan [20]; Lacker [32]; Budhiraja, Dupuis, and Fischer [12]. The upper bound requires compactness of the finite-particle Schrödinger potentials. To obtain it, we use tools from diffusion calculus, notably Bakry–Émery Γ-calculus, Fisher information estimates, and a Poincaré inequality for the target law. A second structural ingredient is permutation symmetry. Although finite-dimensional 

3duality yields particle-dependent potentials, the dual functional is permutation-invariant and concave; averaging over all labels can therefore only improve the dual value. Consequently, the limsup argument depends only on the empirical average of the particle potentials, for which the Sobolev estimates yield compactness. This allows us to identify a limiting Schrödinger potential and close the limsup inequality. Without the symmetrization step, these uniform Sobolev estimates for the potentials are reminiscent of the gradient estimates in Chiarini, Conforti, Greco, and Tamanini [18]. In this way, our result extends the mean field limit theory for controlled McKean-Vlasov systems [20; 32] to the mean field Schrödinger problem with terminal marginal constraints, and complements the propagation of chaos results for constrained systems in Hernández and Tangpi [28], where convexity assumptions on the target law are imposed. The study of further properties of Schrödinger potentials under McKean-Vlasov dynamics is left for future work. In contrast to Theorem 2.5 and Theorem 2.7, which are proved for non-Markovian McKean-Vlasov dynamics with controlled volatility and possibly non-quadratic costs, the mean field limit result Theorem 2.12 is obtained for the case of the mean field Schrödinger problem with Markovian and uniformly elliptic state dynamics, echoing that, for infinite-dimensional problems, dual attainment is more delicate than the dual representation itself. Indeed, the existence of a dual optimizer cannot be inferred from abstract convex analysis alone, since the usual interior conditions fail in spaces of measures, nor from the regularity of the coefficients. The structure of the mean field Schrödinger problem provides, as previously alluded, exactly what is needed. See Section 4 for details. The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 rigorously introduces the weak control rules, the weak optimal transport constraint, the value function, and the standing assumptions. It also states the main existence, convex duFality, and finite-particle approxi-mation results. Section 3 proves the existence theorem and the convex dual representation, and Section 4 proves the finite-particle convergence theorem for the mean field Schrödinger problem.
