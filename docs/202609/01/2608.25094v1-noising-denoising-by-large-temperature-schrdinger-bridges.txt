> Abstract. In this note, we establish a connection between denoising diffusion models and large temperature dynamic Schr¨ odinger bridges computed with respect to certain reference processes. In particular, we show that in the large temperature regime (i.e. T→+∞), the Schr¨ odinger bridge behaves like a forward time diffusion process over the time interval [0 , T / 2], and approximately like the time reversal of a diffusion process over [ T / 2, T ]. This construction provides a process-level characterization of the gradient flow approximation developed by [CCG23].

1. Introduction 

Noising-denoising diffusion models are a dominant paradigm in generative modeling. Intro-duced and first investigated in the works [SDWMG15, SE19, SSDK +21, HJA20], the idea is the following. First, one begins with samples from a distribution and then runs, given this initial data, a diffusion with a known mixing rate. This is called the noising step. The most popular choice of diffusion is the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) process, 

dX t = − 12 Xtdt + dB t,(OU) which has stationary distribution N (0 , Id), where I d denotes the d × d identity matrix. At training time, one fixes a large time horizon T > 0 and learns via a neural network an approximation to the score function of each marginal time point. At inference time, i.e. when one wishes to generate a sample, one performs a time reversal and transforms samples from the stationary distribution into samples from the initial distribution. This step is called 

denoising .In this note, we argue that the large temperature Schr¨ odinger bridge , a solution to a certain entropy minimization problem (see the definition in (1)), behaves like a noising-denoising diffusion model up to a reasonable precision. As we elaborate in Section 1.1, the large temperature Schr¨ odinger bridge may present both theoretical and computational ad-vantages compared to the dominant noising-denoising diffusion models. For the sake of the Introduction, we will introduce our main ideas on Rd equipped with the OU process defined in (OU). However, our results also extend to the compact manifold setting. Note that denoising diffusion models have been considered in the manifold setting in [DBMH +22]. We will fully introduce those ideas and notation later when they become relevant in Sections 2 and 4. For a rigorous statement and proof, see Theorem 5. 1.1. Schr¨ odinger Bridge and Denoising Diffusion Models. The Schr¨ odinger bridge has been employed in a generative modeling context by works such as [DBTHD21, WJX +21, TMF +24, SDBCD23, Pel23, DBKMD24]. We note that [ZS25, BHCK23] specifically consider the Schr¨ odinger bridge with (OU) reference in their analyses. In general, the Schr¨ odinger bridge possesses a body of theoretical and practical results that may make it a more desirable alternative to the more popular noising-denoising diffusion models in certain contexts. Many of these points are raised in [DBTHD21]. For instance, in the time-reversal denoising step, there is a truncation error arising from the fact that one begins with a sample from the stationary distribution, even though the stationary distribution is never attained in finite time. This issue is not present with the Schr¨ odinger bridge as the noising and denoising steps are performed at the same time. We highlight this comparison specifically in the same marginal case. As mentioned earlier, when μ = ν the construction QT in (5) can be most faithfully interpreted as an approximate noising-denoising process as ∇φT / 2 = I d. In this case, P T and QT are similar to so-called mirror interpolations as defined in [ABVE25, Section 3.3]. This allows us to use algorithms for computing P T to obtain a stochastic interpolation that approximately operates as a noising-denoising model at large temperatures. To compute Schr¨ odinger bridges, there are two algorithms with well-studied convergence theory: the Sinkhorn algorithm (Iterative Proportional Fitting Procedure) [Cut13, SK67], and the Iterative Markov Fitting (IMF) algorithm [SDBCD23, Pel23]. Both algorithms are known to produce exponential convergence (with respect to the number of iterations) under assumptions related to log-concavity of the marginals, especially under large regularization (i.e. large T ). For the Sinkhorn algorithm see [CCGT25, Theorem 1.3, Proposition 1.11] and example gallery therein; for IMF, see [SCD25, Theorems 1 and 2] (although these results only apply to the Euclidean setting). There is a vast literature establishing convergence rates of the Sinkhorn algorithm in more general settings, see the recent works [GN26, CDV26, CDG26, Eck25] and references therein. At the same time, these theoretical convergence results do not guarantee strong performance in practical settings of interest. For instance, the implementation of IMF requires fitting a neural network to compute the Markov projection. Similarly for score-based denoising diffusion models, the ability of the neural network to efficiently estimate the score function is key to guaranteeing strong model performance [CCL +23, CLL23, LLT23, CDS25]. We hope that the theoretical results of this note will encourage more investigation into the comparisons and tradeoffs of using large temperature Schr¨ odinger bridges as opposed to denoising diffusion models in generative modeling applications. 1.2. Proof of Theorem 2. Just as in the proof of Theorem 3, the sum of the two relative entropy quantities is the sum of two terms. The first term is controlled by Proposition 6, and the remaining heat kernel term is bounded by Proposition 9. □
