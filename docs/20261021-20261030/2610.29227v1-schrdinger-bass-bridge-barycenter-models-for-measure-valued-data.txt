ABSTRACT
Barycenter models, based on stochastic and deterministic optimal transport bridges between proba-
bility measures, have proven to be useful for supervised and unsupervised learning for (probability)
measure-valued data. In this paper, we utilize the directional asymmetry and dual drift-diffusion
control arising in the recently proposed Schrödinger-Bass Bridge (SBB) between two measures, and
propose SBB-encoder, SBB-decoder, SBB-symmetric, and SBB-Markovian barycenter models. For
each of these novel models, our first theoretical result establishes the existence of the SBB-barycenter
under mild assumptions. Our second theoretical result characterizes the decoder SBB-barycenter
of Gaussians where we show that for Gaussian input measures, the decoder formulation admits
a Gaussian minimizer, reducing barycenter computation to a finite-dimensional optimization and
yielding a compact algorithm. For general input measures, we derive a hybrid Wasserstein gradient
particle descent scheme for all four formulations using the SBB dual representation. Finally, we
utilize these algorithms for solving distributional-inverse/interpolation problems arising in the context
of spatial images and single-cell RNA sequencing data, where we show that the SBB-decoder and
Markovian barycenter allow us to interpolate between successive time points. Results show that our
method outperforms existing barycenter models for measure interpolation and inference.

INTRODUCTION
Barycenter models, that use metrics and divergences between probability measures, provide a means for averaging
and analyzing (probability) measure-valued data, where each observation may itself be a distribution rather than a
single vector, in both supervised and unsupervised settings. Among the most popular models, are the Wasserstein
barycenter model Xu et al. [2018], Schmitz et al. [2018], Werenski et al. [2022], Cheng et al. [2021] which defines a
geometric average through optimal displacement, and Schrödinger-bridge based formulations which incorporate entropic
regularization into the transport process Mallery et al. [2025]. Other examples include Werenski et al. [2025], Gunsilius
et al. [2024], Bai et al. [2023]. Such models have proved useful whenever one seeks a representative distribution that

summarizes several heterogeneous populations. However, in many applications, the observed distributions are not
simply different realizations of the same population. Rather, they may arise from a common latent source through
a combination of systematic transformation and stochastic dispersion. For instance, multiple cell populations can
correspond to different stages of a developmental process, while several image distributions may share the same latent
shape but differ through translation, rescaling, and spatial diffusion. In such settings, directly averaging the observed
endpoint distributions may fail to recover the underlying latent structure, because the variability introduced by the
observation process is itself part of the geometry that should be modeled. This motivates a barycenter construction that
can distinguish deterministic transport from stochastic spreading. The recently introduced Schrödinger–Bass Bridge
(SBB) provides exactly such a mechanism by jointly controlling the drift and the volatility of a stochastic process
[Henry-Labordère et al., 2026, Hasenbichler et al., 2026]. Unlike the classical Schrödinger Bridge, whose volatility is
fixed, SBB allows the diffusion component to adapt; unlike Bass martingale transport, it does not impose a zero-drift or
convex-order constraint on the endpoints. Building on this flexibility, we introduce SBB barycenter models, in which
a shared probability measure is connected to the input distributions through SBB bridges. Because the SBB cost is
intrinsically asymmetric, the position of the barycenter relative to the input measures carries modeling meaning, leading
naturally to encoder, decoder, symmetric, and Markovian formulations.
Main Contributions.
• We introduce the encoder, decoder, symmetric, and Markovian SBB barycenter formulations and establish
their existence on P2(Rd).
• We derive the marginal first variations of the SBB objective. Using these we derive a hybrid Wasserstein
gradient particle descent scheme that is orientation-specific, to solve these barycenter computation problems
efficiently.
• For the decoder SBB problem with Gaussian distributions, we present a static Gaussian projection result that
reduces the decoder search to Gaussian laws, together with a covariance-action description for Gaussian pairs.
We then specialize the particle update to means and covariances.
• Finally, we provide two applications that showcase the utility of the SBB barycenter models: (1) Recovery
of 2-D measures from geometric deformation and spatial blur; (2) Ancestor reconstruction and held-out
interpolation reconstruction in single-cell RNA sequencing data. We show that SBB models and proposed
methods significantly outperform existing barycenter models/methods and baselines.

RELATED WORK
Wasserstein barycenter and entropic regularization.
Wasserstein barycenters average probability measures through
optimal displacement [Agueh and Carlier, 2011], whose dynamic formulation minimizes kinetic transport energy
[Benamou and Brenier, 2000]. Entropic regularization enabled scalable barycenter algorithms, notably the methods of
Cuturi and Doucet [2014] and the iterative Bregman projections of Benamou et al. [2015]. At the same time, entropic
regularization modifies the underlying averaging geometry: entropy terms that are constant when both marginals are
fixed become nonconstant once one marginal is an optimized variable, and can therefore change the resulting barycenter.
On studying EOT barycenter, debiased Sinkhorn barycenters [Janati et al., 2020a] address the resulting entropic bias,
while Chizat [2023] distinguishes regularization of the transport plans from an additional entropy penalty imposed
directly on the barycenter. Mallery et al. [2025] studies the recovery of marginal measure weights through quadratic
optimization.
In the Gaussian setting, Wasserstein barycenters inherit Gaussian structure with Bures–Wasserstein covariance geometry
[Agueh and Carlier, 2011, Nguyen et al., 2025], and related Gaussian closure and closed-form results are known
for entropic transport [del Barrio and Loubes, 2020, Mallasto et al., 2022, Janati et al., 2020b, Bunne et al., 2023].
Computationally, prior barycenter methods include fixed-point schemes [Álvarez-Esteban et al., 2016], first-order
methods with convergence guarantees Altschuler et al. [2021], Nguyen et al. [2025], and particle descent for regularized
barycenters [Chizat, 2023].
Schrödinger bridges and multi-marginal barycenters.
The Schrödinger problem originates with Schrödinger [1931]
and connects entropy minimization to stochastic control [Dai Pra, 1991, Léonard, 2014]. For a Brownian reference,
the diffusion coefficient is prescribed and the drift is controlled. Multi-marginal extensions already provide stochastic
formulations of distributional averaging: Haasler et al. [2021] study tree-structured transport costs and Schrödinger
problems; Noble et al. [2023] develop TreeDSB and apply it to barycenters through a star-shaped construction; and
Howard et al. [2025] extend bridge matching to tree-structured problems and entropic barycentres. Thus, coupling
multiple distributions through a shared stochastic representation has attracted substantial attention in prior work.

Bass martingales and weak optimal transport.
Bass’s stochastic-integral construction [Bass, 1983] underlies the
martingale Benamou–Brenier problem studied by Backhoff-Veraguas et al. [2020]. Here the drift is fixed to zero,
volatility is optimized, and endpoint feasibility requires convex order. The static formulation belongs to weak optimal
transport, where the cost depends on conditional distributions rather than only on pairs of points. General foundations
were developed by Gozlan et al. [2017] and Backhoff-Veraguas et al. [2019]. This distinction is essential for SBB: its
finite-parameter static cost is also a weak transport cost, but allowing nonzero drift removes the martingale convex-order
restriction on its endpoints [Hasenbichler et al., 2026].
Schrödinger–Bass Bridge.
Joint drift and diffusion control belongs to the broader theory of semimartingale optimal
transport [Tan and Touzi, 2013]. Within this framework, Alouadi et al. [2026b] give a one-dimensional PDE derivation
of the Schrödinger–Bass bridge, while Henry-Labordère et al. [2026] develop its semimartingale formulation, duality,
and coupled bridge characterization. Hasenbichler et al. [2026] establish an equivalent static weak-transport formulation
and a dual-ascent computational scheme. On the computational side, Alouadi et al. [2026a] introduce LightSBB-M for
sample-based pairwise bridge estimation. These works provide the pairwise theoretical foundations on which our work
builds.

APPLICATIONS
Unlike Wasserstein transport, the SBB model can account for stochastic spreading through its volatility component.
Our experiments are organized around this interpretation. We now exploit the asymmetry and directionality of SBB.
We begin with a simple particle example illustrating why the direction of the SBB barycenter matters in Section 7.1.
We then consider two complementary latent-recovery problems for handwritten digits in Section 7.2 and single-cell
RNA sequencing in Section 7.3. Detailed settings of all experiments are in Appendix E. All experiments are run on a
NVIDIA H200.
7.1
Directionality of the SBB Barycenter
Since SBB(µ, ν)̸ = SBB(ν, µ) in general (Section 4), the encoder, decoder, and symmetric formulations need not
produce the same barycenter. Figure E.1-E.3 in Appendix E visualize this effect on two-dimensional particles using
three anisotropic Gaussian distributions as marginals. The same collection of input distributions is used in all three
panels, but the resulting barycenter particles differ in both shape and spread. In this particular example, the decoder
barycenter is more concentrated, while the encoder and symmetric formulations produce visibly different geometries.
The figure is intended only to illustrate the directional nature of SBB; it should not be interpreted as a comparison of
convergence rates or numerical accuracy.
This observation motivates the choice of direction in the applications below. Our objective is to infer a common latent
distribution from several observed, corrupted domains. The natural generative picture is therefore ρ⋆−→ν1, . . . , νN,
where ρ⋆is an unknown source and the νi are its observed endpoints. Consequently, the decoder barycenter ρdec is the
formulation aligned with the latent-recovery task.
7.2

Single-cell developmental trajectory reconstruction
Single-cell RNA sequencing provides population snapshots rather than trajectories of individual cells, since cells
are destroyed during measurement Kester and Van Oudenaarden [2018]. This makes developmental reconstruction
naturally a distributional problem: at each time t, the cell population is represented by an empirical measure Pt in
gene-expression space, and the goal is to infer how these distributions evolve. We consider the Waddington-OT iPSC
reprogramming dataset Schiebinger et al. [2019], which contains a dense 18-day time course of mouse embryonic
fibroblast reprogramming. Cells were collected at 39 time points approximately every 12 hours (every 6 hours between
days 8 and 9), yielding more than 2.5 × 105 high-quality cells. The original work infers ancestor–descendant couplings
and shows that iPSC, epithelial, trophoblast, and neural lineages share a common MET-like ancestry until approximately
day 8.

neural populations as descendant measures ν1, . . . , νK and compute their ρdec. The recovered barycenter is interpreted

formulation can better exploit the two-sided temporal structure when reconstructing intermediate states.

CONCLUSION
We introduced Schrödinger–Bass Bridge barycenters as a directed framework for averaging measure-valued data while
accounting jointly for systematic transport and stochastic dispersion. Exploiting the intrinsic asymmetry of SBB, we
formulated encoder, decoder, symmetric, and Markovian barycenters. We develop the theory of existence, a Wasserstein
gradient particle scheme, and Gaussian analysis. The spatial-MNIST and single-cell RNA experiments show that our
method improves latent recovery beyond different baselines. Future work includes establishing convergence guarantees
for the particle scheme and exploring applications of SBB barycenters (including encoder and symmetric model also) in
a broader range of domains.

MEAN–COVARIANCE IMPLEMENTATION OF AFFINE PARTICLE UPDATES
This section gives the additional computational specialization of the particle scheme. The premise is that the pairwise
oracle supplies affine update, as in the Gaussian dynamics of Section 6.
D.1
The general affine update
Let the current measure have mean m and covariance S, and suppose its chosen descent velocity is
u(x) = b + C(x −m).
The particle update X+ = X + ηu(X) can be written as
X+ = m + ηb + (Id + ηC)(X −m).
Taking its expectation gives
m+ = m + ηb.
(D.1)
Subtracting this mean from X+ gives X+ −m+ = (Id + ηC)(X −m). Hence
S+ = (Id + ηC)S(Id + ηC)⊤.
(D.2)
These are algebraic identities for any input law with a finite second moment. When the input is Gaussian, the output is
also Gaussian, since the update is affine. For a finite cloud, they are exact for the empirical covariance computed with
normalization 1/M.

viewpoint as a modification of the spatial barycenter experiment of Cuturi and Doucet [2014].
Latent sources and observations.
We construct the experiment around a latent-source recovery task. We select one
approximately centered clean image from each digit class as the latent source ρ⋆. From each latent source, we generate
N ∈{32, 64, 128} corrupted observations, using N = 32 as the default configuration. The original train/test split is
