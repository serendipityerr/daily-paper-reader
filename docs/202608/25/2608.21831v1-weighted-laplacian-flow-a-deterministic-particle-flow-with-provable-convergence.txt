# Weighted Laplacian Flow: A Deterministic Particle Flow with Provable Convergence 

## Weiye Gan, Tangjun Wang, Zuoqiang Shi 

Abstract 

Sampling from a target probability density is a fundamental task in statis-tics, machine learning, and scientific computing. We introduce weighted Lapla-cian flow, a deterministic particle-flow method that transports samples from a tractable initial density to a target density known up to normalization. The method evolves the logarithmic density ratio between the target and the trans-ported distribution and constructs the particle velocity by solving a weighted Poisson equation associated with the target density. This design avoids the need to choose a kernel and enables direct control of particle weights along the flow. We establish the global well-posedness of the proposed PDE sys-tem and prove that the transported density converges to the target density in both L∞ distance and Kullback–Leibler divergence. Under a sublinear forcing condition, the method achieves exact convergence in finite time. Numerical experiments on multimodal, heavy-tailed, and ten-dimensional targets demon-strate that weighted Laplacian flow can perform long-range mass transport, overcome energy barriers.

# 3 Global Well-posedness 

The proof of well-posedness is organized into three steps: local existence, global con-tinuation, and uniqueness. The main difficulty is that (8) couples a nonlinear trans-port equation for the logarithmic density ratio ω with a weighted Neumann problem for the potential ϕ. We therefore do not solve the two equations simultaneously. Instead, on a short time interval, we freeze one variable at a time and construct a fixed-point map. More precisely, given a potential ϕ, its velocity field v = ∇ϕ generates a char-acteristic flow, along which the second equation in (8) determines ω. This defines 6the transport operator T1. # 6 Conclusion 

In this work, we introduced weighted Laplacian flow (WLF), a deterministic particle-flow framework for sampling from a target density known up to a normalization constant. A substantial part of the paper was devoted to the analytical foundations of this coupled system. On a bounded smooth domain with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions, we established global well-posedness under the stated regularity assumptions. Local existence is obtained by alternating between the characteristic transport problem for ω and the weighted elliptic problem for ϕ, and applying a Schauder fixed-point argument. The key step in extending the local solution globally is a maximum-principle estimate that preserves the L∞ range of ω. Uniqueness follows from stability estimates coupling the difference of the elliptic velocity fields with the difference of the transported log-density ratios along characteristics. The convergence mechanism is also transparent at the level of ω. Monotonicity of f preserves the ordering of log-weights along characteristics and contracts the oscillation, osc ω(t) = max x ω(t, x ) − min x ω(t, x ). Consequently, ω becomes spatially constant asymptotically. Mass conservation then identifies the limiting constant with zero. This yields convergence of the transported density to the target in both L∞

distance and Kullback–Leibler divergence. Moreover, when the forcing has sublinear power-law behavior near the origin, the oscillation vanishes in finite time, giving exact finite-time convergence at the level of the continuous PDE. The numerical experiments support the modeling picture suggested by the anal-ysis. The experiments on multimodal and heavy-tailed targets show that the global elliptic velocity can produce long-range mass transport and move probability mass across low-density regions, while the ten-dimensional experiment indicates that the same mechanism can be realized without a tensor-product grid. Several directions are particularly important for future work. From the modeling perspective, the forcing term can be generalized beyond the purely local relaxation 

F = F (ω). Langevin diffusion corresponds formally to the dissipative forcing 

Fdiff (ω) = 1

p∇ · (p∇ω),

21 which suggests studying hybrid dynamics of the form 

eF (ω) = 1

p∇ · (p∇ω) + λF (ω),

with λ controlling the balance between diffusive exploration and deterministic long-range transport. A systematic analysis should determine how this balance affects well-posedness, convergence rates, metastability, robustness to initialization, and the finite-time relaxation mechanism. On the computational side, the main challenge is to solve the weighted Poisson equation in high dimensional space. The present mesh-free scheme avoids a tensor-product grid and, through the Deep Ritz formulation. Nevertheless, the weighted Poisson equation is still solved at every time level, and the accuracy of both the potential network and the ω-network directly affects the velocity used to transport particles. Developing scalable high-dimensional solvers for the weighted Poisson equa-tion is therefore central to making WLF practical beyond low dimensions.
